Cheap VPS vs free VPS: when paying is worth it

Updated 2026-11-13 · Data from vendor official pages

Short answer: free VPS suits zero budget with card, capacity-hunting, and reclaim rules; cheap VPS (from $21.99/year) buys certainty: no tasks, no reclaim, same-rate renewal, rebuild any time. The test is simple: if one day of downtime costs your service more than $22/year, pay; if it is practice, bots, or you don’t mind the hassle, use free. The two are not opposites: start free to practice, then move to cheap annual plans once the need is proven — the most common path.

The real cost of free

Free is not zero-cost: Oracle needs capacity hunting (Out of capacity is routine) and guarding against 7-day idle reclaim; trial credits need expiry tracking and machine handling before expiry; task hosts need ongoing tasks. Counting that time, $21.99/year (about $1.83/month, converted) buys certainty — worth it for many people.

Combination strategy

The most common path: practice on a free VPS and validate the need (Oracle, GCP always-free, or the InstaBox sandbox), then move services you want to run long-term to a cheap annual plan. Running both in parallel is also common: free machines for experiments, paid machines for production. Task hosts vs $21.99/year annual: a task host costs $0 but needs ongoing time and carries reclaim risk; $21.99/year buys a year of certainty — choose by what your time is worth.

FAQ

Is a free VPS enough?

Enough for practice, running bots, and light services — but accept the card requirement, capacity hunting, and reclaim rules. Paid is recommended for production services and anything that must not go down.

Is $21.99/year worth it?

If one day of downtime costs your service more than $22/year, yes — it buys certainty: no tasks, no reclaim, same-rate renewal.
