Oracle vs GCP: picking one of the two always-free VMs

Updated 2026-11-13 · Table data from vendor official pages (verified 2026-11-12)

Short answer: picking between the two always-free VMs: Oracle has far bigger specs and traffic, but has idle reclaim and capacity problems and is Arm-based; GCP is low-hassle but small, with only 1 GB outbound per month. Both require a card.

How to choose

  • Specs and traffic: pick Oracle. 2 OCPU / 12 GB and 10 TB outbound traffic are far beyond what GCP’s e2-micro free allowance offers — but accept the Arm architecture (check software compatibility), idle reclaim, and capacity hunting.
  • Low-hassle stability: pick GCP. No official idle reclaim rule, and the region and traffic limits are clearly written — but e2-micro is small, and 1 GB outbound per month does not suit traffic-heavy uses.
  • Both: many users hold both free accounts and split the work (e.g., Oracle runs services, GCP is the backup). Follow each vendor’s terms; Oracle allows one free account per person.

FAQ

Which free VPS is better, Oracle or GCP?

It depends on use: Oracle has far bigger specs and outbound traffic (2 OCPU / 12 GB, 10 TB/month) but has idle reclaim and capacity problems and is Arm-based; GCP e2-micro is small with 1 GB outbound per month, but has no official idle reclaim rule. Both require a card. Verified 2026-11-12.

What is the difference between the Arm and x86 free machines?

Oracle A1 is Arm-based; GCP e2-micro is x86. Some software (especially closed-source and Windows-ecosystem software) differs in Arm support — confirm the software you want to run has an Arm build or runs correctly before deploying; Oracle also offers 2 AMD-based E2 Micro instances.
