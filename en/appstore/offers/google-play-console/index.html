Fees

Registration costs a US$25 one-time fee. Mastercard, Visa, American Express, Discover (US only), and Visa Electron (outside the US only) credit or debit cards are accepted; Google Play does not accept prepaid cards; card acceptance varies by location. Processing a Play developer account application can require a valid government ID and credit card, both under the legal name; when that information is found invalid, the registration fee is not refunded. Annual renewal is not mentioned on the pages read — check the official page.

Enrollment steps and account types

Enrollment requires an active Google Account and an age of 18 or older. Enrollment steps run: register an account, accept the Developer Distribution Agreement, pay the registration fee, choose an account type, and verify developer identity information. Account types cover personal and organization accounts. An individual developer can enroll with a personal account; additional organization requirements such as a D-U-N-S number are not covered in the pages read — check the official page.

Closed testing rules

Google Play requires personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023, to test apps before those apps become eligible for distribution on Google Play; until the testing requirements are met, Production and Pre-registration features in Play Console stay disabled. The requirement: run a closed test for the app with a minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days. At the time of the production access application, at least 12 testers must be opted in to the closed test and must have been opted in continuously for the 14 days before the application. When a tester opts out and opts back in later, the 14 days must be consecutive to count. Internal testing is optional, with builds usually available to testers within seconds of joining; closed testing starts after app setup is complete; open testing becomes available after production access is granted.

Production access application

After the criteria are met, the Dashboard in Play Console carries an Apply for production action, with three sections of questions covering the closed test, the app, and production readiness. After submission, Google reviews the application and emails the account owner when review completes; review usually takes seven days or less, but can occasionally take longer. An app can require further testing, with reasons including fewer than 12 opted-in testers or insufficient tester engagement during testing. General release review time is not stated on the pages read — check the official page.

Compliance notes

Developers carry responsibility for full compliance with Google Play developer content policies before submitting for production access; review is not a debugging step, and a non-compliant submission leads to rejection, review delays, and a longer appeal process. Attention points include app content and functionality, target audience and content rating, functional reliability, and test credentials (valid, working login credentials for reviewers to test the app fully).

Service fees

Other markets (until global rollout): developers enrolled in the 15% service fee tier pay 15% for the first $1M (USD) revenue earned each year and 30% for earnings above $1M (USD) revenue each year; subscriptions pay 15% for automatically renewing subscription products, regardless of revenue earned each year. New structure for Australia, the European Economic Area, Japan, the United Kingdom, and the United States (regional rollout dates June 30, 2026 for EEA/UK/US and September 30, 2026 for AU/JP): Standard tier at 10% plus a 5% billing fee for auto-renewing subscriptions, 20% plus a 5% billing fee for other transactions on new installs, and 25% plus a 5% billing fee or 20% for external web links for other transactions on existing installs; participants in the Play Games Level Up and Apps Experience programs pay lower rates (15% plus 5% or 20% plus 5%). Alternative billing systems in South Korea and India reduce the rate by 4%. The new-structure table row for the first $1M of annual earnings shows only the auto-renewing column (10% plus a 5% billing fee) , with the other two transaction columns unstated; rates for those cells — check the official page.

China mainland filing

The Google official pages read do not mention China mainland ICP filing or app filing requirements — check the official page.

Differing accounts

Tester count: several second-hand guides describe 20 testers at the introduction of the November 2023 rule, later reduced to 12; the current official page text states 12 only, with no change history. A second-hand article in Chinese describes a separate $99-per-year Google Play Console paid plan for personal and organization accounts; the official pages read state only the US$25 one-time registration fee, with no mention of that annual plan. The official page governs in both cases.

Questions

What happens when a closed test tester opts out?

At the time of the production access application, at least 12 testers must be opted in to the closed test and must have been opted in continuously for the 14 days before the application. When a tester opts out and opts back in later, the 14 days must be consecutive to count.

Is the registration fee refundable?

Processing a Play developer account application can require a valid government ID and credit card, both under the legal name; when that information is found invalid, the registration fee is not refunded.

Does Google Play charge an annual fee?

The pages read state only the US$25 one-time registration fee and do not mention annual renewal — check the official page.
