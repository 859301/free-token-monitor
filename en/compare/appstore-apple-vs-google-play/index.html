Fee structures differ

Apple membership bills per membership year, with waivers limited to nonprofits, accredited educational institutions, and government entities; Google registration bills once, with waiver terms unstated on the pages read. Registration fees stand at 99 USD per membership year and a US$25 one-time fee respectively, on different billing cycles.

Individual account gates differ

Both stores accept individual enrollment. Apple individual enrollment shows the individual legal name as the seller name, and organization enrollment requires a D-U-N-S number and related materials; a new Google personal account must complete closed testing with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for at least 14 days, with Production and Pre-registration features disabled until the requirement is met.

Review timeline measures differ

The Apple measure covers submission review completion shares, while the Google measure covers production access application review; the two measures cover different stages and are not directly comparable. General release review time for Google Play is not stated on the pages read — check the official page.

Service fee measures differ

The Apple Small Business Program reduced rate stands at 15%, with the standard rate figure stated only as “standard commission rate” on the page read — check the official page. Google other-market rates stand at 15% for the first $1M under the 15% tier, 30% above $1M, and 15% for subscriptions; new-structure markets (Australia, European Economic Area, Japan, United Kingdom, United States) follow a separate table, with individual cells on the official page.

Questions

How do the Apple and Google registration fees differ?

The Apple Developer Program costs 99 USD per membership year, with prices varying by region; Google Play Console registration costs a US$25 one-time fee. The billing cycles differ, so the figures are not directly comparable.

What must a new personal account do before publishing on Google Play?

A personal developer account created after November 13, 2023 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously for at least 14 days, then apply for production access. The Apple pages read state no closed-testing headcount requirement of that kind.

Can the two review timelines be compared directly?

No. The Apple measure covers submission review completion shares (typically at least 50% in less than 24 hours, 90% in less than 48 hours), while the Google measure covers production access application review (usually 7 days or less); the two measures cover different stages.
