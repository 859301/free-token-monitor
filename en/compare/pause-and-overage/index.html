Pause and overage compared

Updated 2026-11-14 · Table data from vendor official pages (verified 2026-11-13)

Short answer: pause, sleep, shutdown, and charging are four different overage behaviors, and each vendor’s official site writes them differently: Supabase writes pause (1 week inactive), Neon writes scale to zero and compute pause (data kept), Turso writes upgrade or blocking, Firebase Spark writes shutdown and Blaze writes no hard cap possible; overage behavior for Upstash and MongoDB Atlas is not stated on the official pages read.

Four behaviors compared

Pause (Supabase): a project pauses after inactivity; the pricing page writes paused. Sleep (Neon scale to zero): compute drops to zero after idle time, data is kept. Shutdown (Firebase Spark): exceeding any plan limit disables the app, preventing further usage and charges. Pay-as-you-go (Firebase Blaze, Upstash after a card is entered): usage beyond the free allowance is billed by usage, and Blaze cannot set a hard usage cap. The Turso FAQ states overage requires upgrade or blocking: either upgrade to a paid tier, or usage is blocked.

Does inactivity delete the database?

Neon states overage keeps data. The Supabase pricing page writes pause, with no deletion rule stated. Deletion rules for Turso, Upstash, MongoDB Atlas, and Firebase are not stated on the official pages read — check the official page. Pausing is not deletion: pause and sleep keep data; deletion rules follow the specific terms on each vendor’s official site.
