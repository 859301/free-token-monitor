Published 2026-11-15 · Updated 2026-11-15 · Verified 2026-11-14

Per-font vs uniform OFL

Google Fonts has no single whole-library license: the FAQ states that licenses differ per font, most commonly OFL, some under the Apache license or the Ubuntu Font License. Source Han Sans and Source Han Serif share the same SIL OFL 1.1 full text with identical conditions. OFL restrictions must not be written as whole-library Google Fonts restrictions.

Logo use differences

The Google Fonts FAQ expressly states that Google Fonts can be used within any logo; Source Han LICENSE.txt files do not address logo text use — check the official page. Use of a font in logo text does not equal trademark registration.

Modification and renaming

Source Han modified versions must not use a Reserved Font Name (unless explicit written permission is obtained) and must not be distributed under another license. Google Fonts fonts can also be modified with a font editor, subject to the license conditions of the font in use.

Document exception

Source Han OFL text states that the requirement to keep the font under this license does not apply to any document created using the font or a derivative. Posters, documents, and websites made with Source Han fonts are not bound by OFL redistribution conditions.

FAQ

Can all three be used commercially?

Yes. Google Fonts can be used in commercial products and even included in commercial products for sale, under conditions set by each font license; Source Han Sans and Source Han Serif allow commercial use, modification, and redistribution under the OFL.

Can Source Han fonts be used in a logo?

LICENSE.txt does not address whether logo text made with Source Han fonts is restricted — check the official page; do not read it as allowed or prohibited. Google Fonts expressly allows use within any logo.

Do Source Han Sans and Source Han Serif share the same license?

Yes. Both LICENSE.txt files carry the same SIL OFL 1.1 full text with identical conditions.
