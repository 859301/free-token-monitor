Different units, aligned first

A QStash message, a Workflow step, an Inngest execution, and a Trigger.dev compute second with an invocation cost are separate units. The same workload consumes different unit counts on each service, so comparison stays within one unit at a time.

Delay and timeout

QStash allows a 7-day maximum delay and a 15-minute maximum HTTP response duration; Inngest allows a 2-hour maximum step duration and a 30-day maximum run length; Trigger.dev runs have no maximum duration, and waits past 5 seconds are not billed; the Workflow maximum delay is not stated on the pricing page read.

Retries and dead-letter queues

QStash retries 3 times by default, with backoff starting at 12 seconds, and failed messages go to the DLQ, retained for 3 days; Workflow retries count as extra steps, and failed runs are recorded in the DLQ; Inngest steps handle retries automatically, with the count unstated; Trigger.dev retry rules are not stated on the pricing page read. The QStash pricing page carries two statements on retry billing, shown both ways on the detail page.

Choosing

For message-based delivery with schedules and delays, QStash applies; for multi-step workflows with sleeps and event waits, Workflow and Inngest apply; for long-running tasks under per-second billing, Trigger.dev applies.

FAQ

Can the four free amounts be compared directly?

No. QStash counts messages, Workflow counts steps, Inngest counts executions, and Trigger.dev counts compute seconds plus an invocation cost; units differ, so cross-unit ranking does not apply.

Which delay is longest?

QStash allows a 7-day maximum delay; Inngest allows one sleep up to 1 year, with total sleep within a run on the Free plan capped at 30 days; the Workflow maximum delay is not stated on the pricing page read.
