Two statements on retry billing

The QStash pricing page carries two statements on whether retries are billed, shown both ways:

  • The machine-readable pricing page opens with: messages only are charged, retries are free (original wording: You are only charged for messages — retries are free.).
  • The FAQ on the same page states: each delivery attempt counts as one message, the initial delivery counts as one message, and each retry is counted as an additional message, with an example of 1 failure plus 1 successful retry billed as 2 messages.

Estimating allowance under the FAQ wording means retries consume additional messages; under the top-of-page wording, retries do not. The pricing page does not state which wording governs.

Retries and backoff

Failed deliveries retry 3 times by default; non-2XX responses trigger retries; delays between retries grow with each attempt, capped at 1 day each; up to 10 redirects are followed.

An endpoint can respond 489 with the Upstash-NonRetryable-Error: true header to disable retries and send the message to the dead-letter queue (DLQ).

Schedules and queues

Maximum schedule resolution is 1 minute, with timezone support (for example CRON_TZ=America/New_York). The Free tier allows up to 10 active schedules and 10 queues, queue parallelism 2 and maximum parallelism 10, a 7-day maximum delay, a 15-minute maximum HTTP response duration, 3-day DLQ retention, and 3-day log retention.

A schedule firing every minute produces 1,440 initial deliveries a day, above the 1,000-a-day free allowance (arithmetic, retries excluded).

Overage and paid tiers

Daily message and monthly bandwidth limits are soft limits, applying only on consistent overuse; short spikes are not blocked immediately. Continued overuse brings a contact about an upgrade first; with no contact and sustained overuse, the API may return 429 errors. Paid tiers: Pay as You Go $1 per 100K messages, bandwidth past 50 GB a month $0.05/GB; Fixed 1M $180/month; Fixed 10M $420/month.

Things to watch

  • Conflicting retry-billing wording: the top of the pricing page states retries are free, while the FAQ on the same page states each delivery attempt counts as one message, with retries counted additionally
  • 3 retries by default, with backoff starting at 12 seconds and each delay capped at 1 day
  • A schedule firing every minute produces 1,440 initial deliveries a day, above the 1,000-a-day free allowance (arithmetic, retries excluded)
  • An HTTP response taking longer than 15 minutes is aborted
  • No SLA and no encryption at rest on the Free tier

FAQ

Is 1,000 messages a day enough for an every-minute schedule?

No. A schedule firing every minute produces 1,440 initial deliveries a day, above the 1,000-a-day free allowance (arithmetic, retries excluded).

Are retries billed?

The pricing page carries two statements: the top states messages only are charged and retries are free; the FAQ on the same page states each delivery attempt counts as one message, with each retry counted additionally, and gives an example of 1 failure plus 1 successful retry billed as 2 messages. The pricing page does not state which wording governs.

How long can a message be delayed, and where do failed messages go?

The maximum delay is 7 days. After 3 retries by default, a message that still fails goes to the dead-letter queue (DLQ), retained for 3 days.
