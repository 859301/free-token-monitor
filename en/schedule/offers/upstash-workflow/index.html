How steps are counted

A step is one call from the QStash server to the Workflow URL or an external URL. context.run, context.sleep, context.sleepUntil, and context.waitForEvent generate 1 billed step each; context.call and context.invoke generate 2 billed steps each; each step in a parallel run costs 1 extra step; retries count as extra steps.

Under those rules, a workflow with parallel branches and repeated retries consumes far more steps than the step count alone suggests.

Overage and failure handling

Daily step and monthly bandwidth limits are soft limits; short spikes do not block step executions immediately; continued overuse brings a contact about an upgrade, and over time workflow runs may fail with quota errors. When the daily step limit is reached, QStash starts returning errors, the SDK throttles and retries after a short delay, and a run fails into the DLQ once the retry limit is reached.

A request body over the size limit at trigger time returns HTTP 412, and the SDK marks the run as failed and records it in the DLQ; oversized responses between steps also fail the run into the DLQ.

Relationship to QStash

Workflow is built on QStash, and step metering follows the QStash delivery model. QStash Free tier delay, DLQ retention, and log retention figures do not transfer to Workflow; the Workflow pricing page read does not state those figures — check the official page.

Overage and paid tiers

Daily step and monthly bandwidth limits are soft limits; short spikes do not block step executions immediately; continued overuse brings a contact about an upgrade, and over time workflow runs may fail with quota errors. Paid tiers: Pay as You Go $1 per 100K steps; Fixed 1M $180/month; Fixed 10M $420/month.

FAQ

What is one step?

A step is one call from the QStash server to the Workflow URL or an external URL.

How are parallel steps and retries counted?

Each step in a parallel run costs 1 extra step, retries count as extra steps, and context.call and context.invoke count as 2 steps each.

What happens past 1,000 steps a day?

The daily step limit is a soft limit; short spikes do not block step executions immediately; at the limit, QStash returns errors, the SDK throttles and retries, and a run fails into the DLQ once the retry limit is reached; sustained overuse may bring an upgrade contact or quota-error failures.
